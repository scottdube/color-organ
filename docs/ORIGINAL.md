# The original Radio Shack unit — catalog evidence

## IDENTIFIED: Radio Shack 28-4009 "Color Organ Kit"
1975 catalog p.103, "NEW FOR 75", $24.95. Found by Scott 2026-09-20 in the
1975 catalog at radioshackcatalogs.com — one of the two years missing from the
worldradiohistory archive, which is exactly where he predicted it would be.

Catalog text, verbatim on the points that matter:
- "Deluxe - You Control the Color Intensity!"
- "**12 high-intensity light sources** behind a prismatic 'starburst lens'
  for an 'endless' depth you can get lost in."
- "Easy to assemble." (a KIT — this is what 42-3001 and 28-137 each failed)
- "Handsome wood-grained cabinet, **18x11-1/2x5**". For 120 VAC."

Resolves all three open questions at once: it is a kit, it has the prismatic
starburst lens, and the lamp count of 12 is now catalog-confirmed rather than
recollection. 42-3001 and 28-137 below are kept as the ruled-out candidates.

### Design consequence: the lens wants POINT sources, not diffusion
The X-shaped starbursts in the catalog photo ARE the lens acting on each lamp.
An earlier pass in this project worried about 12 lamps reading as hot spots
through a diffuser and sized cabinet depth to avoid that. Wrong problem: the
original deliberately turned each lamp into a star. The front is a prismatic
starburst lens, NOT a flat fluorescent-style diffuser, and the replica should
use a prismatic/lenticular panel that produces the same effect.

### Colour layout — read off the catalog photo, not stated in text
The 12 starbursts appear as 3 columns x 4 rows, and the colours look
DISTRIBUTED across the grid rather than grouped one colour per column. If so,
each channel's 4 lamps are scattered over the panel. Read off a small catalog
photo; worth confirming against a better image before committing to wiring.

### Controls: ROTARY, not slide
28-4009's catalog entry says only "Deluxe - You Control the Color Intensity!"
It does NOT state the control type. Scott recalls rotary pots on 28-4009 and
that is the only direct evidence about that unit, so the replica uses rotary.

Slide pots are documented ONLY on the siblings: 28-137 ("Slide-Pot Brilliance
Controls for Each Channel") and 28-138 ("Sliding bass, treble & midrange
intensity controls plus Master control"). An earlier pass carried that detail
across to 28-4009 and called sliders "matching the original" — unsupported.
The count and function DO carry across and are unchallenged: three per-channel
intensity controls plus a master.

## ORIENTATION: the 18 in dimension is HEIGHT, not width
Scott, 2026-09-20, and corroborated by the 1978 catalog ad photo, which shows
the cabinet standing PORTRAIT — taller than wide. Radio Shack listed these
height-first. Getting this backwards inverts which unit is "the wider one" and
sent an earlier pass down a false trail:

- 42-3001 = **11-1/4 in wide** x 18 in tall x 5 deep
- 28-137  = **8-7/8 in wide**  x 18 in tall x 4-7/8 deep

So 42-3001 is the WIDER of the two, and it is also the prismatic-lens one.
Scott's recollection ("the wider one with the prismatic diffuser") is
self-consistent and points at 42-3001.

Source: Radio Shack consumer catalogs, scanned at worldradiohistory.com.
Extracted 2026-09-20 via pdftotext over the OCR layer (not read off images).
Archive holds 1973, 1974 and 1977 only — **no 1975 or 1976 catalog exists there**,
so those two years are unsearched, not clear.

## 28-137 — "Three-Channel Color Organ Kit"  (ruled out on width)
1973 and 1974 catalogs, $29.95 both years. Gone by 1977.

- **A kit.** "Assemble it in an evening — easily!"
- **Per-channel controls**: "Slide-Pot Brilliance Controls for Each Channel!" /
  "You Control Each Color — Red, Blue And Green!"  Slide pots, not rotary.
- **Hardwired, speaker level**: "1/4 watt min. input, 80 watt max."
- **White flat front panel**: "the white front panel becomes a vortex of
  flashing, pulsating colors" — a diffuser, NOT the faceted lens on 42-3001.
- Bass / treble / midrange -> red, blue, green.
- Woodgrain finish. **18 x 8-7/8 x 4-7/8 in** (1974). 1973 OCR reads "18x8x4%",
  same cabinet, OCR dropped the fraction.
- 120 VAC, ship wt 10 lbs.

## 42-3001 — "3-Channel Color Organ"  ← believed to be Scott's
1973, 1974 and 1977 catalogs, $29.95 unchanged across all three.

- **Fully assembled**, not a kit.
- Hardwired: "just connect to speaker leads" / "Connect to any speaker".
- **Prismatic lens** (1973/74), called a **"starburst" lens** by 1977 — a faceted
  3-D lens, not a flat white diffuser.
- Wood-grain / walnut-grained vinyl veneer.
- **18 x 11-1/4 x 5 in** (1973/74); listed as 18 x 11-1/2 x 5 in (1977).
- U.L. listed, 120 VAC.

## 42-3014 — the later one Scott found in a 1978 ad
Voice-actuated, built-in sound sensors, no speaker connection. 18-1/4 x 11-5/8
x 4-3/4 in, $29.95. Later than the unit Scott built, and NOT hardwired — this
is why the microphone reads as an addition to HIS unit but not to the line.
Same ad also lists 42-3013, a diamond wall-hanging model, 24-1/4 x 13 x 3-1/2 in
— the likely source of the remembered "24" dimension.

## What the catalogs do NOT say — still unverified
- **Lamp count and grouping.** No catalog text states 12 lamps or 4-per-channel.
  That is Scott's recollection only. An enthusiast thread independently
  describes a 3-channel Radio Shack unit using "ordinary large Christmas type
  bulbs", which corroborates the lamp TYPE and the channel split, nothing more.
- **Where the slide pots physically sit.** Scott says rear of the cabinet.
- **Lamp pitch / internal layout.** Unknown. On a PORTRAIT 11-1/4 x 18 face,
  12 lamps land either as 3 cols x 4 rows (3.75 in x 4.5 in pitch, channels
  reading as vertical stripes) or 4 cols x 3 rows (2.8 in x 6 in, horizontal
  stripes). The 1978 ad photo of the later 42-3014 reads as horizontal bands,
  but that is a different model and is weak evidence for this one.
- **"Kit".** Scott built his as a kit; the catalogs describe 42-3001 as fully
  assembled in 1973, 1974 and 1977. Either memory blends the two products, or a
  kit form existed in the unsearched 1975/1976 catalogs. UNRESOLVED.
- **The circuit.** Not recovered. Mains triac design, inferred from the U.L.
  listing, the 120 VAC operation and the speaker-lead input (which implies an
  input transformer for isolation). Inferred, not measured.
